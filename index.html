Are More Expensive Games Rated More Highly?

Wending He, Sergio Iribe, Mandy Xu, Jason Yu

Prototype Checkpoint Writeup:

1. What have you done so far?

So far, our group has selected the Top 1000 Steam Games dataset from Kaggle and filtered it down to 898 games with review data. We established our research question, "Do More Expensive Games Get Better Reviews?", and created several static visualizations to explore relationships between game prices, review scores, and genres. We have also developed and published an initial GitHub Pages website featuring an interactive scatter plot that compares game prices with positive review percentages. The visualization includes genre filters and price-range selections that allow users to focus on specific groups of games. Additionally, users can hover over individual data points to view information such as the game's title, genre, price, review score, and total number of reviews. Our initial findings suggest that more expensive games do not necessarily receive better reviews, and we plan to expand on these findings through additional visualizations and a structured narrative.

2. What will be the most challenging of your project to design and why?

One of the most challenging aspects of our project will be expanding our current prototype into a complete interactive data visualization website. Although we have already implemented a scatter plot with genre filters, price-range selections, and hover interactions, we still need to incorporate our additional visualizations into a cohesive presentation. Another challenge will be organizing the website into a three-stage narrative that allows users to understand our initial findings before exploring the data independently. Lastly, we must balance the amount of information displayed with the overall readability and usability of the website will be important as we continue developing the final project.
